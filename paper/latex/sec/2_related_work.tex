\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\subsection{Efficient Convergence under Limited Optimization Budgets}
The cost of radiance-field reconstruction can be reduced either by making each
update cheaper or by reducing the number of updates needed to obtain a useful
representation. Explicit grids and compact encodings accelerated neural-field
optimization, while 3DGS made full-resolution differentiable rendering
practical through an explicit primitive representation~\cite{kerbl20233dgs}.
Depth supervision can further reduce ambiguity and training time when only a
small set of observations is available~\cite{deng2022dsnerf}. Within 3DGS,
Taming 3DGS controls primitive growth and improves the optimization kernels
under explicit resource limits~\cite{mallick2024taming3dgs}. Photo-SLAM uses
geometry-based densification and Gaussian-pyramid learning to improve
photorealistic mapping under incremental optimization~\cite{huang2024photoslam}.
Recent geometry-aware online mapping revisits scale initialization and
densification because offline 3DGS heuristics are brittle under short online
budgets~\cite{geometryOnline2026}.

These works establish that initialization, representation capacity, and the
cost of an optimization step strongly affect attainable quality. Our focus is
complementary: we study the joint \emph{trajectory} of photometric and
geometric quality after observations arrive. We therefore compare methods not
only at a fixed endpoint, but also at matched elapsed time and matched completed
updates. This operational definition is important in a growing map, for which
there is no single static objective whose asymptotic convergence fully
describes online behavior.

\subsection{Online 3DGS SLAM and Observation Allocation}
Neural dense SLAM systems introduced continuous optimization of a scene
representation during camera tracking. iMAP selects rays using information
gain~\cite{sucar2021imap}, NICE-SLAM improves scalability with hierarchical
features~\cite{zhu2022nice}, and Co-SLAM combines coordinate and sparse
parametric encodings with global ray sampling~\cite{wang2023coslam}.
DROID-SLAM instead estimates pose and dense depth through recurrent updates and
differentiable dense bundle adjustment~\cite{teed2021droid}; this frontend is
particularly useful for initializing Gaussian maps from dense geometric
estimates.

3DGS-SLAM systems differ in how tracking, geometry, and appearance mapping are
coupled. Gaussian Splatting SLAM and SplaTAM track against Gaussian maps and
select local or overlapping keyframes for mapping~\cite{matsuki2024monogs,
splatam2024}. Photo-SLAM decouples conventional localization from progressive
photorealistic Gaussian mapping~\cite{huang2024photoslam}, RTG-SLAM restricts
updates to unstable primitives for scalable reconstruction~\cite{rtgslam2024},
and Splat-SLAM jointly refines poses, depth, and Gaussians~\cite{splatslam2024}.
VIGS-SLAM combines a DROID-style recurrent visual--inertial frontend with a
Gaussian mapping backend~\cite{vigsslam}. These systems primarily use
keyframes and local visibility to determine which observations participate in
mapping, reflecting the close historical coupling between tracking and map
optimization.

The closest observation-allocation work is CaRtGS, which identifies uneven
keyframe re-optimization in a growing keyframe pool and assigns additional
iterations using per-keyframe loss~\cite{cartgs}. Active-view methods such as
FisherRF instead choose which camera observation should be acquired from
expected information gain~\cite{jiang2024fisherrf}. Our setting assumes a
fixed causal stream: the question is which already observed images should be
used by the mapper, and how a fixed number of updates should be distributed
among them. We expand the mapping-view set beyond tracking keyframes according
to completed mapping work and use update counts as an inexpensive coverage
signal. Entropy-Regularized View Sampling favors less frequently selected
views while discouraging an overly concentrated sampling distribution. It
does not estimate information gain or guarantee non-convex 3DGS convergence.
Its use of sampling without replacement is motivated by
random-reshuffling methods~\cite{shamir2016without,mishchenko2020random}, while
the changing online objective prevents directly inheriting their fixed-sum
convergence guarantees.

\subsection{Geometric Convergence and Floater Suppression}
Photometric reconstruction is geometrically under-constrained because many
opacity and depth configurations can explain similar colors. DS-NeRF treats
sparse depth as a distributional constraint along each ray~\cite{deng2022dsnerf},
and Mip-NeRF~360 regularizes the distribution of rendering weights with a
distortion loss~\cite{mipnerf360}. For Gaussian reconstruction, 2DGS uses
surface-oriented disks together with depth-distortion and normal-consistency
terms~\cite{huang20242dgs}, while PGSR introduces planar depth and multi-view
geometric consistency~\cite{chen2024pgsr}. These methods improve geometric
accuracy, but are chiefly evaluated in offline reconstruction where long
optimization schedules and global access to posed images are available.

Floater-removal methods intervene in primitive selection or in the coupling
between geometry and appearance. TrimGS prunes Gaussians using multi-view
contribution and geometric criteria~\cite{fan2024trimgs}. EFA-GS emphasizes
low-frequency structure to reduce floating artifacts caused by poor
initialization~\cite{wang2025efags}. StableGS attributes persistent floaters to
weak opacity gradients under color supervision and separates geometry and
appearance opacity paths so that stronger depth constraints do not erase
visual detail~\cite{stablegs}. Online systems also use scale, opacity, and
visibility constraints: RTG-SLAM distinguishes stable opaque primitives from
low-opacity ones~\cite{rtgslam2024}, and CaRtGS combines adaptive optimization
with opacity regularization~\cite{cartgs}.

Our geometry objective is designed for the shorter horizon and restricted
compute of online mapping. Rather than globally relocating or pruning every
suspect primitive, the current Carve instantiation uses causal depth to ask a
one-sided question: is a Gaussian's opacity supported in space known to be
free? Contradicted opacity is suppressed, while unobserved space and possible
surfaces behind the first hit are left unconstrained. Because this operation
can improve free-space precision by deleting structure, we evaluate it jointly
with surface accuracy, completeness, and held-out appearance. This distinguishes
fast geometric convergence from aggressive opacity removal.
