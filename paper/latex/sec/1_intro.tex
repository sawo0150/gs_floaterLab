Building geometrically accurate and photorealistic 3D maps online remains an important challenge in autonomous robotics.
Among the representations used for 3D mapping, including point clouds, meshes, and surfels, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)~\cite{kerbl20233dgs} has gained attention for enabling dense, photorealistic maps with real-time rendering from novel viewpoints~\cite{matsuki2024monogs,huang2024photoslam}.
The ability to generate visual observations from a map offers opportunities for vision-based perception and decision-making~\cite{chen2025splat, intro_Shorinwa2025splatmover}, with the development of vision-language-action (VLA) models~\cite{zitkovich2023rt2, intro_black2024pi0} further motivating maps that can provide visual inputs.
To achieve this level of quality, the representation requires careful optimization of numerous parameters—including position, scale, rotation, color, and opacity—for millions of primitives.
Since this optimization process is computationally intensive and relies on dense multi-view constraints, 3DGS frameworks are predominantly trained offline using pre-collected datasets.
Autonomous robots, however, need to construct and update maps as they navigate and explore their environments, providing the spatial context beyond the current field of view to support timely planning and action.

To support such continuous map updates, Gaussian SLAM systems combine camera tracking with incremental map expansion and refinement~\cite{matsuki2024monogs,huang2024photoslam}.
For newly observed regions, efficient pose and per-pixel depth estimation~\cite{teed2021droid} enables dense Gaussian initialization from estimated scene surfaces~\cite{zhang2025hislam2}.
Tightly coupling visual and inertial measurements further improves these pose estimates used to align incoming observations with the growing map, supporting more consistent incremental reconstruction~\cite{vigsslam}.
During online refinement, depth supervision~\cite{zhang2025hislam2,vigsslam} and Gaussian shape regularization~\cite{matsuki2024monogs} help maintain geometric consistency as the map's appearance is optimized.
To improve the efficiency of this refinement, systems employ multi-resolution learning~\cite{huang2024photoslam} and adaptive allocation of optimization steps across keyframes~\cite{cartgs}.
The observations used for these updates are typically drawn from local keyframe windows~\cite{zhang2025hislam2}, with some systems additionally sampling historical keyframes to continue refining previously mapped regions~\cite{matsuki2024monogs,vigsslam}.

Despite these advances, rapid convergence to photorealistic maps with accurate geometry remains challenging under online constraints.
Offline 3DGS~\cite{kerbl20233dgs} benefits from repeated optimization over a fixed, pre-collected dataset, with dense multi-view coverage constraining both appearance and geometry.
Shuffling these views further mixes their gradient contributions to support optimization over the full observation set~\cite{bottou2012sgd}.
Online mapping instead distributes limited updates over a growing set of observations.
For robots exploring with head-mounted cameras, changes in viewing direction can make egocentric observations of individual regions brief and intermittent.
Restricting map optimization to tracking keyframes~\cite{huang2024photoslam} can leave some scene regions with sparse viewpoint coverage, while local windows~\cite{zhang2025hislam2} limit the range of views sampled for refinement.
Random replay of past keyframes~\cite{rtgslam2024} expands this range, but older keyframes can still accumulate more updates than newer ones.
With few viewpoints per region, photometric supervision alone can leave Gaussian geometry underconstrained~\cite{sparsegs}.
Repeated updates may fit the available images without resolving ambiguities in scene geometry.
Efficient online refinement therefore requires balanced updates across diverse views together with geometric guidance that remains effective with few viewpoints.



% 방법론 문단 1개로 줄이기
To address these challenges, we introduce ROGO-SLAM (Rethinking Online Gaussian Optimization), a framework that adapts photometric optimization and geometric supervision to streaming observations for fast photometric convergence and geometrically consistent mapping.
To broaden photometric supervision beyond tracking keyframes, we progressively build a set of non-keyframe RGB images for appearance refinement while retaining geometric supervision from keyframes.
Our Optimization-Guided View Set Growth controls when images enter this set based on the number of photometric optimization steps performed on the map, allowing repeated map refinement between additions.
Within this growing set, Entropy-Regularized View Sampling (ERVS) selects images for photometric optimization, favoring those used in fewer previous optimization steps while retaining randomness in image selection.
To provide geometric guidance even with few viewpoints per region, our Carve loss uses currently available keyframe depth estimates to penalize Gaussian opacity in the free space between the camera and observed surfaces.
We apply the gradients of this loss only to opacity, reducing Gaussian contributions in the identified free space without driving changes in Gaussian positions or shapes.

We evaluate ROGO-SLAM's ability to build photorealistic, geometrically consistent maps from streaming observations under real-time constraints.
Our experiments use the public RGB--IMU benchmarks RPNG~\cite{chen2023monocular} and UTMM~\cite{sun2024mm3dgs}.
To assess mapping when individual scene regions are visible only briefly or intermittently, we also include egocentric sequences we recorded with Project Aria glasses~\cite{engel2023aria}.
Comparative evaluation shows that ROGO-SLAM improves rendering quality on images excluded from training while operating in real time.
Convergence analysis further shows that our method reaches comparable rendering quality with fewer optimization steps on the Gaussian map.
These results highlight the importance of adapting Gaussian optimization to the observations received so far and the available computational budget during online mapping.

Our contributions are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We present ROGO-SLAM, an online Gaussian mapping framework that adapts view management and geometric supervision to streaming observations for fast photometric convergence and geometrically consistent reconstruction.
    \item We propose Optimization-Guided View Set Growth and Entropy-Regularized View Sampling to control when non-keyframe RGB images enter training and distribute photometric updates more evenly across them while retaining randomness in image selection.
    \item We introduce a Carve loss that uses currently available keyframe depth estimates to suppress Gaussian contributions in free space, with gradients applied only to opacity.
\end{itemize}
