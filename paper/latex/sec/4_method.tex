
\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

Our goal is to use the information carried by every causal observation without
weakening the geometric backbone of an online 3DGS mapper.  A SLAM keyframe is
selected for tracking and triangulation and carries RGB--D, normal, and
BA-refined pose constraints.  An intermediate RGB frame is much more frequent
and supplies continuous appearance evidence, but its interpolated pose and
lack of depth make it a poor substitute for a geometric keyframe.  We therefore
do not merge both observations into one undifferentiated replay pool.  Instead,
we assign them separate geometric and photometric service roles and account for
their completed render work inside the mapper's existing causal budget
(\S\ref{ssec:growth}).  Within the photometric role, entropy-regularized
count balancing distributes the available service over the growing dense-view
pool (\S\ref{ssec:ercb}).  Finally, causal ray-space supervision constrains
free-space violations using only depth evidence that has already arrived
(\S\ref{ssec:carve}).


% ---------- 3.1 ----------
\subsection{Role-Aware Causal View Service}
\label{ssec:growth}

Let $\mathcal K_t$ be the keyframes available at time $t$ and let
$\mathcal D_t$ be the admitted intermediate RGB observations.  A member of
$\mathcal K_t$ receives the native mapping objective, including geometric
supervision and topology statistics.  A member of $\mathcal D_t$ receives an
RGB objective through a photometric update.  Its image and pose are formed only
after the two bracketing keyframes have arrived, so neither membership nor
optimization can use a future keyframe.  This role separation is important:
dense RGB augments repeated appearance refinement, but never permanently
replaces a particular RGB--D carrier.

The dense pool grows according to work that has actually completed.  We use a
one-view \emph{primary} photometric service as the conservative admission
clock and reserve the multi-view flexible iteration below for repeat service.
This prevents a large repeat batch from immediately purchasing an equally
large set of unseen views.  Let $S_{\mathrm P}(t)$ be the number of completed
primary services, and let $N_0$ be the initial dense-pool size.  With one
global admission cost $\kappa$, the admitted set obeys
\begin{equation}
    \kappa\left(|\mathcal D_t|-N_0\right)
    \leq S_{\mathrm P}(t),
    \label{eq:view_growth_constraint}
\end{equation}
and hence
\begin{equation}
    N_{\max}(t)
    =
    \min\left(
        M(t),
        N_0+\left\lfloor\frac{S_{\mathrm P}(t)}{\kappa}\right\rfloor
    \right),
    \label{eq:view_growth_bound}
\end{equation}
where $M(t)$ is the number of causally bracketed dense observations available
by time $t$.  Attempted, preempted, and flexible repeat steps mint no admission
credit.  The rule uses neither the final sequence length nor a scene-dependent
frame cutoff.

We next reallocate, rather than add, render work.  Consider a native mapping
call $q$ with $L_q$ Adam iterations and let $C_q$ be the number of views that
its final iteration would render after applying the native view cap.  We
protect the first $L_q-1$ iterations as complete geometric iterations and
expose only the final iteration as one flexible service quantum.  It offers
$C_q$ photometric render credits, giving cumulative debt
\begin{equation}
    d_q^{\mathrm P}
    =
    \sum_{r\leq q} C_r^{\mathrm{offer}}
    -
    \sum_{r\leq q} C_r^{\mathrm{done}}.
    \label{eq:photometric_service_debt}
\end{equation}
The flexible iteration becomes photometric only if $L_q>1$, the causal dense
pool contains at least $C_q$ distinct views, and $d_q^{\mathrm P}\geq C_q$.
Otherwise, the unmodified geometric iteration runs and the unused credit
remains as debt.  A successful photometric iteration commits exactly $C_q$
services; an interrupted iteration commits none.  Consequently, each call
retains exactly $L_qC_q$ physical renders and $L_q$ Adam steps while preserving
at least one full geometric iteration.  No view is duplicated to fill a batch,
and an immature dense pool cannot silently reduce the work budget.
The mapper's existing one-view auxiliary appearance slot is likewise assigned
to a dense repeat rather than to another keyframe; because it is repeat service,
it does not mint primary admission credit.

We partition the Gaussian parameters as
$\Theta=(\Theta_{\mathrm G},\Theta_{\mathrm A})$, where
$\Theta_{\mathrm G}$ contains position, scale, rotation, and topology state,
and $\Theta_{\mathrm A}$ contains the spherical-harmonic appearance.  The
geometric role applies the native loss and updates both groups.  The
photometric role applies RGB supervision only to $\Theta_{\mathrm A}$; its
geometry gradients and densification statistics are discarded.  Thus,
intermediate frames continuously refine appearance without moving or replacing
the RGB--D geometry carriers.


% ---------- 3.2 ----------
\subsection{Entropy-Regularized Photometric Service}
\label{ssec:ercb}

Because $\mathcal D_t$ grows throughout the stream, uniform random sampling
gives early observations more opportunities to be revisited than late
observations.  We therefore balance only the completed photometric service
counts.  Let $n_i$ be the number of committed photometric renders of dense view
$i$, let $N_s=|\mathcal D_t|$, and let

\begin{equation}
\bar n = \frac{1}{N_s}\sum_i n_i.
\end{equation}

We measure count dispersion relative to its current mean,
\begin{equation}
\Phi(\mathbf n)
=
\frac{1}{2N_s\bar n}
\sum_{i=1}^{N_s}
(n_i-\bar n)^2.
\label{eq:count_dispersion}
\end{equation}
where $T=\sum_i n_i=N_s\bar n$.  With the pool held fixed, one additional
service of view $i$ changes the potential by
\begin{equation}
\Delta_i\Phi
=
\Phi(\mathbf n+\mathbf e_i)-\Phi(\mathbf n)
=
\frac{n_i}{T+1}+C_s,
\label{eq:count_dispersion_increment}
\end{equation}
where $C_s$ is shared by all candidates.  Rather than deterministically
choosing the least-served view, we retain stochasticity through the
entropy-regularized objective

\begin{equation}
\min_{\mathbf p\in\Delta}
\sum_i p_i \Delta_i\Phi
-
\tau H(\mathbf p),
\label{eq:entropy_sampling_objective}
\end{equation}
where $H(\mathbf p)=-\sum_i p_i\log p_i$ and $\Delta$ is the
probability simplex.  Its Gibbs optimum is
\begin{equation}
p_s(i)
=
\frac{
\exp\!\left(-\Delta_i\Phi/\tau\right)
}{
\sum_j \exp\!\left(-\Delta_j\Phi/\tau\right)
}.
\label{eq:entropy_sampling_general}
\end{equation}
Substituting Eq.~\eqref{eq:count_dispersion_increment} removes the term shared by all views and yields
\begin{equation}
p_s(i)
=
\frac{
\exp(-\beta_s n_i)
}{
\sum_j \exp(-\beta_s n_j)
},
\qquad
\beta_s
=
\frac{1}{\tau(T+1)}.
\label{eq:entropy_view_sampling}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, with a fixed global $\gamma=1/\tau$,
\begin{equation}
p_s(i)
\propto
\exp\!\left(-\frac{\gamma n_i}{T+1}\right).
\label{eq:normalized_ercb_probability}
\end{equation}
This is the normalized-variance form: its effective balancing strength
decreases as completed service accumulates, rather than silently recovering
the constant-strength raw-variance sampler.

At each flexible iteration, we draw $C_q$ distinct dense views sequentially
without replacement, renormalizing Eq.~\eqref{eq:normalized_ercb_probability}
over the remaining candidates.  Newly admitted zero-service views form a
first-service floor; after that floor is empty, the Gibbs law determines the
draw.  The complete batch is transactional: selection counts advance only
after the owning Adam step succeeds, and a preempted proposal is restored
without prepaying service.  Round-robin uses the same causal pool, batch size,
first-service rule, and commit semantics, changing only the Gibbs energy to
zero; it is therefore a work-matched ablation of the balancing policy.

\subsection{Causal Ray-Space Geometry Supervision}
\label{ssec:carve}

Photometric supervision constrains the composited color but does not uniquely
determine where opacity lies along a ray: a premature Gaussian can reproduce
the observed color while occupying known free space. A conventional rendered
depth loss is also insufficient because it first compresses all contributors
to one mean depth, allowing separated front and back modes to cancel. We
therefore supervise the first-termination process induced by Gaussian
compositing using only depth observations available at the current time. This
gives two complementary constraints: \emph{Carve} suppresses termination before
the observed surface, while \emph{Hit} places termination mass at that surface.

\paragraph{Ray termination.}
For a depth-ordered set of Gaussian contributors to ray $r$, let
$\alpha_i^0$ be the base opacity, $G_{ri}^{\mathrm{2D}}$ the projected
Gaussian value at the pixel, and $z_{ri}$ its camera-space depth. The effective
alpha, incoming transmittance, and termination weight are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ray_termination}
  a_{ri}=\alpha_i^0G_{ri}^{\mathrm{2D}},\qquad
  T_{ri}=\prod_{j<i}(1-a_{rj}),\qquad
  w_{ri}=T_{ri}a_{ri}.
\end{equation}
The remaining no-hit mass is
$T_{\mathrm{end},r}=\prod_i(1-a_{ri})$, and
$\sum_iw_{ri}+T_{\mathrm{end},r}=1$. We equivalently introduce a categorical
first-termination index $I_r$ with
$p(I_r=i\mid\Theta)=w_{ri}$ and
$p(I_r=\varnothing\mid\Theta)=T_{\mathrm{end},r}$. This is an interpretation
of deterministic alpha-compositing mass, not a stochastic renderer.

For each multi-view-verified keyframe ray, VIGS provides a BA-refined depth
$D_r$. Its latent first-surface depth $Z_r^\star$ is represented by an
external posterior
\begin{equation}\label{eq:surface_posterior}
  \begin{aligned}
  q_r(z)&=p(Z_r^\star=z\mid\mathcal O_r)
  =\mathcal N(z;D_r,\tau_r^2),\\
  F_r(b)&=\Phi\!\left(\frac{b-D_r}{\tau_r}\right).
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal O_r$ denotes the front-end observations. We stop gradients
through $D_r$ and $\tau_r$, keeping this evidence independent of the Gaussian
map parameters $\Theta$.

\paragraph{Free-space likelihood (Carve).}
Given a surface at $z$ and a safety margin $m_r$, the target event is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:carve_event}
  \mathcal E_r^{\mathrm F}(z)
  =\{\text{no model termination before }z-m_r\}.
\end{equation}
Its conditional likelihood is exactly the transmittance
$p(\mathcal E_r^{\mathrm F}(z)\mid Z_r^\star=z,\Theta)
=T_\Theta(r,z-m_r)$. Taking the posterior expectation of its conditional NLL
gives
\begin{align}
  \mathcal L_{\mathrm{C\text{-}ENLL}}
  &=\frac{1}{|\mathcal R_D|}\sum_{r\in\mathcal R_D}\sum_i
    \gamma_{ri}\big[-\log(1-a_{ri})\big], \label{eq:carve_enll}\\
  \gamma_{ri}
  &=p(Z_r^\star>z_{ri}+m_r\mid\mathcal O_r)
    =1-F_r(z_{ri}+m_r), \nonumber
\end{align}
where $\mathcal R_D$ is the set of verified depth rays. Thus, a Gaussian is
penalized in proportion to the posterior probability that it lies in observed
free space. With geometry detached, minimizing Eq.~(\ref{eq:carve_enll})
monotonically decreases only its opacity.

Alternatively, marginalizing the compatibility event before taking the NLL
gives
\begin{align}
  P_{\mathrm{C\text{-}MARG},r}
  &=\int q_r(z)T_\Theta(r,z-m_r)\,\mathrm dz \nonumber\\
  &=T_{\mathrm{end},r}+\sum_iw_{ri}F_r(z_{ri}+m_r), \label{eq:carve_marg}\\
  \mathcal L_{\mathrm{C\text{-}MARG}}
  &=-\frac{1}{|\mathcal R_D|}\sum_{r\in\mathcal R_D}
  \log(P_{\mathrm{C\text{-}MARG},r}+\epsilon). \nonumber
\end{align}
Equation~(\ref{eq:carve_marg}) is the exact NLL of the stated compatibility
event under an external $q_r$, whereas Eq.~(\ref{eq:carve_enll}) is its
posterior-expected conditional NLL. Jensen's inequality gives
$\mathcal L_{\mathrm{C\text{-}MARG}}
\leq\mathcal L_{\mathrm{C\text{-}ENLL}}$, so the latter is more conservative.
Both objectives permit a no-hit solution and therefore remove free-space
violations without, by themselves, creating surface coverage.

\paragraph{First-hit likelihood (Hit).}
Carve specifies where termination should not occur; Hit additionally explains
the measured surface. Conditioned on termination at Gaussian $i$, we use the
forward observation model
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hit_observation}
  k_{ri}=p(D_r\mid I_r=i,\Theta)
  =\mathcal N(D_r;z_{ri},\sigma_r^2),
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_r$ is calibrated measurement noise, not Gaussian thickness.
Marginalizing the latent termination index yields
\begin{align}
  p(D_r,I_r\neq\varnothing\mid\Theta)
  &=\sum_i p(D_r\mid I_r=i,\Theta)p(I_r=i\mid\Theta) \nonumber\\
  &=\sum_iw_{ri}k_{ri}, \label{eq:hit_likelihood}\\
  \mathcal L_{\mathrm{HIT}}
  &=-\frac{1}{|\mathcal R_D|}\sum_{r\in\mathcal R_D}
  \log\!\left(\sum_iw_{ri}k_{ri}+\epsilon\right). \nonumber
\end{align}
Writing $A_r=\sum_iw_{ri}$ decomposes each ray loss into
$-\log A_r-\log\sum_i(w_{ri}/A_r)k_{ri}$: the first term demands a finite hit,
and the second aligns the complete termination distribution with $D_r$.
Consequently, near-surface components gain termination mass, premature
occluders lose opacity when they block a better downstream explanation, and
components with nonzero mixture responsibility receive position gradients
toward $D_r$. Unlike Carve, the canonical Hit objective therefore optimizes
opacity, position, and projected shape.

\paragraph{Non-overlapping Carve--Hit objective.}
Adding global Carve and Hit losses independently can count the same front
transmittance twice because every $w_{ri}$ already contains $T_{ri}$. For an
exact factorization, define a free-space boundary $B_r=D_r-m_r$, an upper
surface boundary $U_r$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:carve_hit_partition}
  \mathcal F_r=\{i:z_{ri}<B_r\},\qquad
  \mathcal S_r=\{i:B_r\leq z_{ri}\leq U_r\}.
\end{equation}
Let
$T_r^{\mathrm F}=\prod_{i\in\mathcal F_r}(1-a_{ri})$ and reset
transmittance inside the surface set,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local_hit_weight}
  \widetilde w_{ri}^{\mathrm S}
  =a_{ri}\!\!\prod_{\substack{j\in\mathcal S_r\\z_{rj}<z_{ri}}}
  (1-a_{rj}).
\end{equation}
Since $w_{ri}=T_r^{\mathrm F}\widetilde w_{ri}^{\mathrm S}$ for
$i\in\mathcal S_r$, the restricted first-hit NLL factorizes as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:carve_hit}
  \mathcal L_{\mathrm{C+H}}(r)
  =-\log T_r^{\mathrm F}
  -\log\!\left(
  \sum_{i\in\mathcal S_r}\widetilde w_{ri}^{\mathrm S}k_{ri}
  +\epsilon\right).
\end{equation}
The first term suppresses opacity only in front of the surface boundary; the
second creates and aligns termination mass only in the local surface region.
Resetting transmittance at $B_r$ prevents double penalization. Replacing the
hard first term with Eq.~(\ref{eq:carve_enll}) gives an uncertainty-aware
composite objective, but no longer the exact factorization in
Eq.~(\ref{eq:carve_hit}).

\paragraph{Online integration.}
We evaluate these objectives only on spatially balanced, multi-view-verified
depth rays. The existing rasterizer already computes
$(z_{ri},G_{ri}^{\mathrm{2D}},a_{ri},T_{ri},w_{ri})$ in its front-to-back
contributor loop; we accumulate the required likelihood in the same pass and
combine it with the mapping loss before a single backward pass. The additional
work is therefore linear in the number of selected ray--Gaussian pairs and
does not require a second full-frame render. Carve stops gradients through
depth, ordering, and footprint and updates opacity only, avoiding unreliable
position updates across sorting discontinuities. Hit retains full gradients
for surface contributors. The factorized objective applies these routes
separately to $\mathcal F_r$ and $\mathcal S_r$.

With the unchanged VIGS mapping loss $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{base}}$, the three
candidate objectives are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:geometry_objectives}
  \begin{aligned}
  \mathcal L_{\mathrm{map}}^{\mathrm C}
    &=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{base}}+\lambda_{\mathrm C}\mathcal L_{\mathrm C},\\
  \mathcal L_{\mathrm{map}}^{\mathrm H}
    &=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{base}}+\lambda_{\mathrm H}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{HIT}},\\
  \mathcal L_{\mathrm{map}}^{\mathrm{C+H}}
    &=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{base}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{C+H}}\mathcal L_{\mathrm{C+H}},
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal L_{\mathrm C}$ denotes either
$\mathcal L_{\mathrm{C\text{-}ENLL}}$ or
$\mathcal L_{\mathrm{C\text{-}MARG}}$ as selected by the controlled ablation.
