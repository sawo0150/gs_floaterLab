\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
\ac{3DGS} is getting attention from its photorealistic view synthesis and widely utilized in vision-based autonomous agents~\cite{intro_zitkovich2023rt2}. However, its computationally intensive optimization is not suitable for online-mapping, which is often required for active perception and action generation. Furthermore, weak geometric convergence induces floaters in free space and vague object surface boundaries. Recent works bring \ac{SLAM} systems to enable online learning, but inefficient view selection and insufficient consideration about geometry are degrading the mapping performance.

%First, view selection for \ac{SLAM} pipeline is not the most effective observations for Gaussian-map refinement. Admitting every incoming frame spreads limited updates across more images, while uniform sampling leaves differences in accumulated updates uncorrected as new frames enter training. 
When integrating 3DGS into SLAM pipelines, using only SLAM keyframes for 3DGS optimization is insufficient due to the complexity of the scene representation. Thus, incorporating suitable frames into the training set while effectively allocating optimization steps across frames is important. We address this by incrementally adding non-keyframe images as mapping optimization progresses and introducing Entropy-Regularized View Sampling (ERVS) to prioritize frames that have received fewer updates, balancing new observations with continued refinement.

 Limited number of viewpoints on the scene during online mapping pose another challenge: photometric optimization can converge to a local minimum that reproduces the observed images but yields inaccurate scene geometry.
Depth-based losses provide geometric supervision, but their effect depends on which Gaussian parameters receive their gradients. Directly optimizing Gaussians upon their positions leads to discontinuity of gradients, resulting from the discrete ordering in 3DGS rendering. Using the shape as a constraint doesn't resolve the visibility of the free space Gaussians.
Therefore we introduce a causal, depth-guided Carve loss that directly suppresses Gaussian opacity in free space.
By routing its gradients exclusively to opacity, we prevent changes in free space Gaussian positions or shapes from compensating for this penalty.

Our contributions are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We formulate an online 3DGS mapping framework that targets fast photometric and geometric convergence under limited computation.
    \item We introduce Optimization-Guided View Set Growth and ERVS to coordinate frame admission and update allocation with mapping progress.
    \item We design an opacity-directed causal Carve loss to suppress Gaussian contributions in observed free space through selective gradient routing.
\end{itemize}

\section{Efficient Online Gaussian Mapping with Reliable Geometry}
\label{sec:method}

Our framework incrementally builds a Gaussian map from streaming RGB images and IMU measurements.
A DROID-SLAM-based frontend~\cite{teed2021droid} provides poses and depths to initialize and grow the Gaussian map.
View Set Growth controls when new images are added to the training set, while View Sampling selects images from this set for each optimization update, and the carve loss adds depth-based geometric supervision to map optimization.

\noindent\textbf{Optimization-Guided View Set Growth.}
Frames between \ac{SLAM} keyframes can provide additional viewpoints and appearance information, but adding them to the training set leaves fewer optimization steps per image on average under a fixed budget.
We therefore control training view set growth by adding one available image after every $\kappa$ optimization steps on the Gaussian map (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}(a)).

\noindent\textbf{Entropy-Regularized View Sampling.}
Images added earlier to the training set have more opportunities for optimization.
We distribute optimization steps more evenly across images while retaining randomness in image selection (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}(b)). We formulate this objective as
\begin{equation}
  \min_{\mathbf p\in\Delta}
  \sum_i p_i\Delta_i\phi-\tau H(\mathbf p).
  \label{eq:view_sampling}
\end{equation}
The first term favors less frequently selected images, while the entropy term encourages randomness in image selection.
Here, $\mathbf p=(p_i)$ contains image-selection probabilities, and $\Delta$ contains nonnegative vectors summing to one.
We measure imbalance by $\phi=\operatorname{Var}(\mathbf n)/(2\bar n)$, where $\mathbf n=(n_i)$ records cumulative selection counts and $\bar n$ is their mean. 
$\Delta_i\phi$ denotes its change after selecting image $i$ once more.
The entropy is $H(\mathbf p)=-\sum_i p_i\log p_i$, with weight $\tau>0$.

\noindent\textbf{Causal Free-Space Carve Loss.}
Depth observations provide evidence of free space between the camera and a surface.
The Carve loss uses these observations to penalize Gaussian opacity in this free space, accounting for depth uncertainty and a margin around the surface (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}(c)).
Its gradients act on opacity without updating Gaussian positions or rotations.

\section{Results and Conclusion}

We evaluate on the public RGB--IMU benchmarks: RPNG~\cite{chen2023monocular}
and UTMM~\cite{sun2024mm3dgs}, and our own dataset collected by wearable glasses ARIA.
Under matched optimization step budgets, average PSNR on images excluded from training exceeds the baseline~\cite{zhu2026vigs} by 1.60, 0.52, and 2.45\,dB, respectively.
Our method requires fewer mapping iterations to achieve comparable rendering quality, enabling efficient online map refinement.
After the same number of iterations, it produces clearer text and finer details than the baseline (Fig.~\ref{fig:photometric_convergence}).

We present an online Gaussian mapping system for efficient optimization of appearance and geometry, achieving faster photometric convergence and higher rendering quality under limited optimization budgets.
Our system introduces view management to control training-set growth and allocate optimization steps across images, and a depth-guided Carve loss to suppress Gaussian opacity in free space.
This approach provides a foundation for online mapping in robots and wearable augmented-reality systems that need reliable maps of their
surroundings during operation, with limited computation and without waiting for offline refinement.
